Four ways cc-control could change how you work

None of these is a nicer layout for chatting with Claude. Each picks a different thing to put at the centre of the screen: the ticket, the steps of your loop, the record of what happened, or what Claude knows. Every one keeps the cockpit rules: local only, keyboard first, keys drawn on screen.

Your loop, as the yardstick

Each path is judged against the way you work today.

Ticket→ Context→ Plan→ Build→ Try it→ Fix rounds→ Ship: PR and review post

"Try it" means smoke testing: starting the app however that repo starts. All four paths share one idea for it: a run recipe per repo or workspace, detected from package.json or compose.yaml and editable, so it works however your team starts its apps.

They share a foundation, and they combine

Needed by all four

  • A hooks bridge: terminal sessions post their events (tool use, questions, stop) to the local server. This is the documented way in, and it needs no special flags.
  • Run recipes for smoke testing, detected from the repo and editable.
  • Ticket import from Jira and Trello into a session's context.

Combinations that work

  • Line + Recorder: the board as home, and a card's drawer opens its lane of the timeline.
  • Line + Runbook: the board for many tickets, the runbook when you open one.
  • Map as the workspace screen of any of the others.

Questions that decide it

  • Will you keep typing to Claude in a terminal? If yes, lean toward 3 or 1.
  • Who is the showcase for: your org's developers, or people who don't write code? The first points to 3, the second to 2.
  • Is the ticket or the session the thing you think in?
