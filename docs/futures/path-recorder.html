Flight Recorder: keep your terminals, and see every step they take

You keep talking to Claude in your terminals, exactly as you do today. The app becomes the instrument panel. It records every step every session takes on one timeline, so you can see what happened, scrub back to any step, compare sessions side by side and undo to a point that worked.

A day with it

  1. 09:00

    You pick up SHOP-142 in Jira and start claude in a terminal in web-app, pasting the ticket like you do today. The SessionStart hook reports it and a lane appears under Storefront within a second. Two older terminals are already recording.

  2. 09:20

    The lane turns violet while Claude plans. You read and approve the plan in the terminal. The recorder keeps the plan as a step you can come back to.

  3. 10:18

    A red block: two cart tests fail. You don't switch windows. By 10:52 the lane is green again, and the inspector shows the one-line fix to the total.

  4. 11:08

    PAY-88 turns amber and pulses. Alt 1 jumps to it. The inspector shows the question next to the diff that led to it. You press 1 and the answer goes to its terminal through the channel.

  5. 11:15

    Files view flags Cart.tsx: SHOP-142 and PAY-88 both changed it. You tell PAY-88, in its terminal, to leave the cart to SHOP-142.

  6. 11:30

    SHOP-151 is flagged ready to try. r runs web-app's recipe: install, seed, dev, then opens localhost:5173. The images are faster, but the product grid now jumps as they load.

  7. 11:35

    You scrub SHOP-151 back to the lazy-loading edit, press u to undo the steps after it, and tell Claude in the terminal what you saw. That is feedback round 1.

  8. 14:00

    Round 2 is green. You commit, push and open the PR from the terminal, and the lane gets a PR marker. Posting to #storefront-reviews stays manual in this path.

Good at

  • Seeing several sessions at once, with each step's type visible from across the room.
  • Finding the step where things went wrong, with its diff and outcome one key away.
  • Undoing back to a step that worked, per session.
  • Catching two sessions editing the same file before it becomes a merge problem.
  • No change at all to how you talk to Claude.

Trade-offs

  • A timeline is dense. Newcomers need a minute to read it.
  • Hooks must be installed once per machine. One command writes them to ~/.claude/settings.json.
  • Answering from the app needs the terminal started with the channel flag. Without it, the app shows the question and t takes you there.
  • Undo in terminal sessions relies on our own snapshots, because file checkpoints are only available to app-run sessions.
  • Plain-language step lines cost a small model call each, or fall back to Claude's own tool descriptions.

Drops

  • Ticket intake. You still copy the ticket into the terminal.
  • Shipping: push, PR and the Slack post. Could come back as an add-on.
  • The in-app message box, modes and number pad.
  • A guided first run for non-developers.
