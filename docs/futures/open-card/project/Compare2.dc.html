What the owner said, 2026-10-02
The app is not a new face for a terminal; it is for getting through many sessions faster, by developers and non-developers. When someone opens a card they should see what they need to make a quick judgment call, do the one thing the card needs (answer, approve, type), and move on to the next card. That means the chat (read, send, approve), the diffs grouped by repo, and starting this card’s apps on their own ports with clear instructions on how to test this one; later, hooks into the team’s own apps to look up and add records and create test data from the card’s context. Claude’s to-do list and the session’s details can stay out of sight. Every card is its own slice (its worktrees, its own app instances), so a card is tested on its own; merging across cards is for later.

What changed from A, B and C
A, B and C all still showed Steps and Session on the page. D to G take the owner at their word: steps and session go behind one link, the chat is always reachable but only F makes it the page, and the diff is grouped by repo everywhere because each card runs its own worktrees and ports. Two things appear that no earlier board had: a “since you last looked” marker (D, E, F), and the test steps that turn a verifier’s fail into a message to Claude (G).
A way to read them together
They are not exclusive. F is a frame: a calm chat with a dock. D’s per-repo diff and G’s test bench are what two of F’s drawers would hold, and E’s queue is a left rail that could sit beside any of them. If one has to be first, F with D’s drawer gives the chat, the diffs by repo and Try it on day one, and leaves room for G and the team’s apps without a redesign.
